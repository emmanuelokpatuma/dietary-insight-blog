Choosing bread with diabetes isn't always easy, and the label doesn't always help.

Diabetes UK suggests choosing granary, pumpernickel or rye bread instead of white and wholemeal, as they tend to raise blood sugar more slowly. Wholemeal, seeded and multi-grain breads add fibre, and adults should aim for 30g a day. Brown bread isn't as high in fibre as wholemeal, and granary isn't always made from wholemeal flour.

Salt matters too. Blood Pressure UK gives the example of two similar loaves where one has 1g of salt per slice and the other half that.

Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "What is the best bread for diabetics in the UK?"

Diabetes UK's advice is to choose granary, pumpernickel or rye bread instead of white and wholemeal, because they tend to raise blood sugar more slowly. It lists rye bread as low GI, and white bread, bagels and some types of wholemeal bread as high GI. Some mixed grain breads with whole grains in them also have a lower GI.

Labels can be confusing. Wholemeal, wholewheat and wholegrain mean basically the same thing. Brown bread isn't as high in fibre as wholemeal, granary may or may not be made from wholemeal flour, and wheat-germ bread is made from white flour, so it isn't wholegrain.

The amount matters more than the type, though: Diabetes UK says the total carbohydrate you eat has the biggest effect on blood sugar. Pairing bread with protein or fat lowers the GI of the meal, and frozen and defrosted bread has more resistant starch than fresh.

If you have high blood pressure, check salt as well. Blood Pressure UK says two similar loaves can contain 1g of salt per slice versus half that.
