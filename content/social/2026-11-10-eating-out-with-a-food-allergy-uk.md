Eating out should be one of life's simple pleasures, but with a food allergy it takes planning.

The rules in the UK help: restaurants, cafés and takeaways must tell you if any of the 14 major allergens are in their food. For takeaways and delivery, the Food Standards Agency says that information must be available both when you order and when the food arrives.

The practical side matters just as much. Allergy UK and Anaphylaxis UK suggest phoning ahead, asking about sauces, oils and shared pans, and choosing a quieter time, because mistakes are more likely when a kitchen is busy.

Where: r/FoodAllergies, or Quora: "How do you eat out at restaurants with a food allergy in the UK?"

In the UK, restaurants, cafés and takeaways must tell you if any of the 14 major allergens are in their food. That can be on the menu or by asking staff, but they don't have to make you a different meal.

What UK guidance suggests:

- Check the menu online and phone ahead. Ideally speak to a manager or head chef about how it's prepared and how they handle cross-contamination.
- Go at a quieter time. Allergy UK says chefs are more likely to make mistakes when it's busy.
- Ask about sauces, glazes, oils, garnishes and shared fryers or griddles, not just the main ingredients. Some sauces may contain wheat flour, and some curries may be thickened with peanut flour or ground almonds.
- Check again when the food arrives. If it has to go back, ask for a freshly made dish, not the allergen scraped off.
- For takeaways, allergen info must be available when you order and when it's delivered. Anaphylaxis UK suggests phoning rather than relying on notes in apps.
- If staff can't answer clearly, the FSA says don't eat there.

If you're prescribed adrenaline, the NHS says carry 2 at all times. If you have signs of anaphylaxis, use it and call 999.
