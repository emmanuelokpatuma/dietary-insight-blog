"Can I still eat fruit?" is one of the most common questions after a diabetes diagnosis. Diabetes UK's answer is reassuring: yes, any fruit you like, as long as it's whole fruit spread through the day.

The detail is in the portion and the format. The sugar naturally found in whole fruit doesn't count as free sugar, but the sugar in juice and smoothies does, which is why both the NHS and Diabetes UK suggest no more than 150ml a day. Dried fruit is easy to overdo too: a portion is about one heaped tablespoon.

Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "What fruits can a diabetic eat?"

Short answer: pretty much any fruit. Diabetes UK says there's no reason people with diabetes can't eat any fruit they like, as long as it's whole fruit spread through the day rather than a big bowl in one go.

A few things that make the difference:

- Portion size. The NHS says one portion is 80g of fresh fruit, for example 1 apple, banana or pear, 2 satsumas, 7 strawberries or a handful of grapes.
- Dried fruit is small. A portion is about 30g, or 1 heaped tablespoon of raisins. The NHS suggests having it at mealtimes because it's sugary and can be bad for teeth.
- Juice and smoothies are the main thing to limit. The sugar in whole fruit doesn't count as free sugar, but the sugar in juice does, and juice has much less fibre. The NHS and Diabetes UK both say 150ml a day maximum.
- Tinned fruit is fine, but pick fruit in natural juice rather than syrup.

Most fruits are low to medium GI, according to Diabetes UK, so they don't hit blood sugar like white bread does. If you're on medication or a specific eating plan, a dietitian can tell you the portions that suit you.
