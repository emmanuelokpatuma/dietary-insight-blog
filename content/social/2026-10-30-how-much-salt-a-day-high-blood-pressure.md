Where: r/hypertension, or Quora: "How much salt should I eat a day with high blood pressure?"

The UK limit for adults is 6g of salt a day, which is about one level teaspoon. That's a maximum, not a target.

The tricky part is that about three quarters of the salt we eat is already in food when we buy it, so cutting back on the salt shaker only goes so far. The big sources are bread, breakfast cereals, processed meat (ham, bacon, sausages), ready meals and takeaways, sauces like soy sauce, gravy and ketchup, and cheese.

A few things that make it easier:
- Check the front-of-pack label. More than 1.5g of salt per 100g is high (red), 0.3g or less is low (green).
- If a label only lists sodium, multiply it by 2.5 to get salt. 0.6g sodium = 1.5g salt, already a quarter of your day.
- Cook from scratch a bit more and flavour with garlic, herbs, lemon, pepper or chilli.
- Go easy on stock cubes and soy sauce, or use reduced-salt versions.

Your GP or practice nurse can tell you what's right for you, especially if you're on blood pressure medication.
