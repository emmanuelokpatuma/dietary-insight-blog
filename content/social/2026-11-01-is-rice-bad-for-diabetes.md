Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "Is rice bad for people with type 2 diabetes?"

Rice isn't banned, but it does raise blood sugar, because it's mostly carbohydrate. Diabetes UK says the total amount of carbs you eat has the biggest effect on your glucose after a meal, so portion size is the main thing to look at.

A few practical points from the Diabetes UK and NHS guidance:

- A portion of cooked rice is roughly what fits in two hands cupped together, around 180g. Weighing it a few times helps you learn what that looks like.
- Cooked white rice has a high GI. Basmati is medium GI.
- Brown rice, brown basmati or quick-cook brown rice are whole grains with more fibre, and wholegrains are usually better for managing blood glucose.
- Eating rice with vegetables, beans or some protein changes how the meal affects you compared with rice on its own.
- Wholewheat couscous, quinoa or bulgur wheat are easy alternatives for variety.

If you take medication that can cause hypos and want to cut carbs right down, it's worth checking with your diabetes team first.

And for leftovers: cool rice within an hour, keep it in the fridge, eat it within 24 hours and only reheat it once.
