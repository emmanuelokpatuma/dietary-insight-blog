Natasha's Law changed UK food labelling on 1 October 2021.

Food that is made and packed on the same premises where it's sold, before you order, is called "prepacked for direct sale". Think sandwiches, salad boxes, pies and wrapped deli cheese. The Food Standards Agency says these must now show the name of the food and a full ingredients list, with the 14 allergens emphasised.

What it doesn't cover matters too. Loose food, food made to order and food bought online or by phone don't carry this label, though allergen information must still be available. Allergy UK's advice is simple: check the label every time, and if in doubt, don't take the risk.

Where: r/FoodAllergies, or Quora: "What is Natasha's Law and what does it cover?"

Natasha's Law came into force on 1 October 2021. It covers "prepacked for direct sale" (PPDS) food: food made and packed on the same premises where it's sold, before you order. Think sandwiches, wraps, salad boxes, pies, soup already in pots and wrapped deli cheese. The Food Standards Agency says these must now show the name of the food and a full ingredients list, with any of the 14 regulated allergens emphasised (bold, capitals, contrasting colour or underlining).

It's named after Natasha Ednan-Laperouse, who died in 2016 after an allergic reaction to an ingredient in a baguette bought at Heathrow Airport.

What it doesn't cover: loose food (like unpackaged cakes or a salad bar), food made to order, and food bought online or by phone. Businesses still have to give you allergen information for those, for example on a menu or by asking staff, and for online orders before you order and on delivery. Food packed in a factory elsewhere has its own full label.

"May contain" labels are voluntary. Allergy UK says there's no law on when they're used, and advice on avoiding them should come from a healthcare professional. Check labels every time you buy, and if in doubt, don't take the risk.
