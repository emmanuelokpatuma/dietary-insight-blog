Where: r/hypertension, or Quora: "What is the DASH diet and does it work for high blood pressure?"

DASH stands for Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. It was designed by researchers at the US National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute in the 1990s, and the British Heart Foundation explains it well.

The basics:

- More fruit and veg (4 to 5 servings of each a day), wholegrains (7 to 8 small servings) and low-fat dairy (2 to 3).
- Nuts, seeds and beans 4 to 5 times a week, lean meat or fish in small portions.
- Salt kept to about 6g a day (2,300mg sodium), or about 4g (1,500mg) in the lower-sodium version, which the BHF says may lower blood pressure further.
- Added sugar under 5 servings a week, alcohol within 14 units a week.

The BHF cites a 2019 review linking DASH to a 5.2mmHg drop in systolic and 2.6mmHg drop in diastolic pressure.

One caution: Blood Pressure UK says if you have kidney disease or take certain blood pressure medicines, a big increase in potassium could be harmful, so check with your doctor first and avoid potassium-based salt substitutes. And don't change any tablets without talking to your GP.
