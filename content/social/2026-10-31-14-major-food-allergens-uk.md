Where: r/FoodAllergies, or Quora: "What are the 14 allergens in the UK?"

UK food law lists 14 major allergens: celery, cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats), crustaceans (prawns, crab, lobster), eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs (mussels, oysters), mustard, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphur dioxide and sulphites (above 10 parts per million), and tree nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, Brazil nuts, cashews, pecans, pistachios, macadamias).

A few things that are useful to know:

- On prepacked food, these allergens must be emphasised in the ingredients list, usually in bold. Check the ingredients, not the front of the pack.
- Since October 2021, food prepacked for direct sale (PPDS) needs a full ingredients list with allergens highlighted.
- Loose food, restaurants and takeaways must give you allergen information too. It can be verbal, but staff must tell you where to find it in writing. Takeaways must provide it before you buy and when it's delivered.
- "May contain" labels are voluntary and warn about possible cross-contamination.
- Peanuts are legumes, so they're listed separately from tree nuts.

If your allergen isn't one of the 14, the FSA's advice is to check the label or ask staff.
