The useful part is knowing what "free sugars" means. It covers sugar added to food and drinks, honey, syrups, and fruit juice and smoothies, even unsweetened ones. Where: r/nutrition, or Quora: "How much sugar should you eat a day in the UK?"

The NHS guideline is that free sugars should make up no more than 5% of your daily energy. In practice that means:

- Adults and children 11+: no more than 30g a day (about 7 sugar cubes)
- 7 to 10 years: 24g (6 cubes)
- 4 to 6 years: 19g (5 cubes)
- 2 to 3 years: 14g (3.5 cubes)
- 1 year: 10g (2.5 cubes)

The key is "free sugars". That's any sugar added to food or drink, plus honey, syrups, and fruit juice and smoothies, even unsweetened ones. The sugar naturally in whole fruit, vegetables and milk doesn't count.

That's why labels can be confusing: the "of which sugars" figure is total sugar, so a plain yoghurt can show up to 8g per serving with none of it free sugar. As a rough guide, the NHS says more than 22.5g of sugars per 100g is high and 5g or less is low.

For context, a can of cola can have as much as 9 cubes, more than the adult limit. Juice and smoothies are best kept to 150ml a day in total, with a meal.
