HbA1c comes up at almost every diabetes check-up, so it helps to know what the number means.

It's a simple blood test that shows your average blood sugar over the last 2 to 3 months. You don't need to fast for it. The NHS uses these ranges as a guide: below 42mmol/mol (6%) is normal, 42 to 47mmol/mol may mean a risk of diabetes, and 48mmol/mol (6.5%) or above may mean diabetes.

Diabetes UK says people with diabetes are entitled to the test at least once a year, and that being unwell, stress and some medicines can all change the result.

Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "What is a normal HbA1c level in the UK?"

HbA1c is a blood test that shows your average blood sugar over roughly the last 2 to 3 months. It works because glucose sticks to red blood cells, which last around 2 to 3 months. That's why it differs from a finger-prick reading, which is just a snapshot.

The NHS gives these ranges as a guide: below 42mmol/mol (6%) is normal; 42 to 47mmol/mol (6% to 6.5%) may mean you're at risk of diabetes; 48mmol/mol (6.5%) or above may mean you have diabetes. If you already have diabetes, Diabetes UK says an ideal level is 48mmol/mol or below, but your team may set a personal target, especially for older people.

You don't need to fast for it. The NHS says people with diabetes are usually tested every 3 to 6 months, and Diabetes UK says you're entitled to at least one test a year.

Results can be affected by illness, stress, some medicines like steroids, and conditions such as sickle cell anaemia or thalassaemia. If your number is high, talk to your GP or diabetes nurse before changing anything.
