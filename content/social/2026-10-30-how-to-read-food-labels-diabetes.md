Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "How do I read food labels when I have diabetes?"

The quickest way in the UK is the traffic-light label on the front of the pack, using the per 100g figures:

- Sugars: 5g or less per 100g is low (green), more than 22.5g is high (red)
- Saturated fat: 1.5g or less is low, more than 5g is high
- Salt: 0.3g or less is low, more than 1.5g is high

Always compare products using the per 100g column, not "per portion". Manufacturers pick their own portion sizes and they're often smaller than what people actually eat.

Then glance at the ingredients. They're listed biggest first, and sugar hides under lots of names: glucose, dextrose, maltose, corn syrup, honey, agave, fruit juice concentrate. A product can use several of these so none appears near the top.

Also skip anything sold as "diabetic" food. It's often no better and can still raise blood sugar.

For context, UK guidance is no more than 30g of free sugars a day for adults (about 7 sugar cubes). Natural sugars in whole fruit, veg and milk don't count towards that.
