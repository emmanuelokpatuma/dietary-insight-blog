People often ask which foods lower blood pressure. The British Heart Foundation's advice is about an overall pattern of eating rather than any single food.

That pattern is simple: more fruit and vegetables (at least five portions a day), wholegrains such as oats, lean protein and low-fat dairy, with salt kept to no more than 6g a day. Blood Pressure UK adds a useful caution: get potassium from food, and if you have kidney disease or take certain blood pressure medicines, check with your doctor before greatly increasing it.

Where: r/hypertension, or Quora: "What foods help lower blood pressure naturally?"

There isn't one magic food. UK heart charities point to an overall pattern of eating:

- Fruit and vegetables: the British Heart Foundation says they all supply potassium, which can help lower blood pressure. Aim for at least five portions a day; Blood Pressure UK says a portion is 80g (e.g. three heaped tablespoons of veg or pulses).
- Wholegrains: oats, brown rice, wholemeal bread. The BHF says the beta-glucan fibre in oats may help.
- Lean protein and low-fat dairy: chicken, fish, eggs, beans, semi-skimmed milk and low-fat yogurt. Calcium is linked to lower blood pressure.
- Beetroot juice: research has suggested it might help, according to the BHF.

Just as important is what you limit: no more than 6g of salt a day (about a teaspoon, and most of it is hidden in bread, cereals, sauces and processed meat), and alcohol well within 14 units a week.

One caution: get potassium from food, not supplements. The BHF says too much can affect heart rhythm, especially with kidney problems or certain water tablets. And keep taking any blood pressure medicine you've been prescribed; talk to your GP before changing anything.
