The UK advice is more encouraging than many expect. Diabetes UK lists plantain as an example of a low GI food, and even suggests it as a change from white rice. Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "Is plantain good for people with diabetes?"

Plantain can still fit into a healthy diet with diabetes. Diabetes UK lists it as a starchy food, alongside potatoes, rice, pasta and bread, so it does raise blood sugar. But it also includes plantain and yam in its list of example low GI foods, which raise blood sugar slowly, and suggests plantain as a change from white rice.

The bigger factor is portion. Diabetes UK says the amount of carbs you eat has a bigger effect on blood sugar than GI alone, and that watching carbohydrate portions is the most important thing.

Cooking matters too. Diabetes UK says ripeness and cooking method can change the GI of a food, and that fat lowers GI (crisps have a lower GI than potatoes cooked without fat), so GI isn't the whole story. Its plantain chips recipe bakes thinly sliced green plantain with 2 tsp of sunflower oil for four servings (26.5g carbs per 162g serving). NHS Healthier Families grills ripe plantain with no oil, or steams it to serve with beans in ewa oloyin.

Pairing plantain with beans, fish or chicken and veg helps, as Diabetes UK says protein, fat and fibre slow the absorption of carbs.
