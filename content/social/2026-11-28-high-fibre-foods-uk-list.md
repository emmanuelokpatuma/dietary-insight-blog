Most of us in the UK are eating about a third less fibre than we should.

Government guidelines say adults should aim for 30g a day. The NHS says the average adult eats about 20g. It also says eating plenty of fibre is associated with a lower risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer.

The good news is that the fixes are ordinary and often cheap: beans and lentils, porridge oats, wholemeal bread, potatoes with the skins on. An NHS example day of wholemeal toast, a lentil pasta lunch and a skin-on baked potato at dinner gets close to 30g.

Where: r/EatCheapAndHealthy, or Quora: "What are the best high fibre foods to eat in the UK?"

UK government guidelines say adults should aim for 30g of fibre a day, and the NHS says most adults only eat about 20g. Children need less: about 15g at 2 to 5, 20g at 5 to 11 and 25g at 11 to 16.

The British Heart Foundation lists these approximate amounts per 100g: red kidney beans 11.1g, toasted wholemeal bread 8.2g, porridge oats 7.8g, dry roasted peanuts 7.6g, boiled lentils 7.4g, boiled peas 5.6g, baked sweet potato 5.2g and baked beans 4.9g.

Easy swaps from NHS advice: wholemeal or granary bread instead of white, wholewheat pasta or brown rice, potatoes with the skins on, pulses in stews and curries, and fruit, oatcakes or unsalted nuts as snacks. On labels, "high fibre" means at least 6g per 100g and "source of fibre" 3g per 100g.

Increase it gradually and drink plenty of water. If you have IBS, Crohn's or another gut condition, the BHF says you may need individual advice.
