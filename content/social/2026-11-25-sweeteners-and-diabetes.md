Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "Are artificial sweeteners OK for diabetics?"

Short answer: for most people, yes. Diabetes UK says low-calorie sweeteners (like aspartame, sucralose, saccharin and stevia-based ones) contain little or no calories or carbohydrate and don't affect blood glucose levels. The NHS says every sweetener approved in Great Britain goes through a rigorous safety assessment, and acceptable daily intakes are set so far below any level of concern that Diabetes UK says it's extremely difficult for most people to reach them. The main exception is aspartame for people with PKU, a rare inherited condition.

A joint statement from the BDA, British Nutrition Foundation and Diabetes UK says swapping sugary drinks for diet versions can help reduce carbs and manage blood glucose after meals, but sweeteners aren't a stand-alone solution, and water is still the ideal drink.

Two things to watch: sugar alcohols (sorbitol, xylitol, maltitol and so on) still contain some carbohydrate and can cause bloating and diarrhoea in large amounts. And Diabetes UK doesn't recommend "diabetic" labelled foods, because they can be as high in fat and calories as normal versions. If you use insulin and aren't sure how to count sugar alcohols, ask your diabetes team.
