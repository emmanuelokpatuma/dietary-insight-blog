Where: r/Cholesterol, or Quora: "Which foods are high in saturated fat and raise cholesterol?"

The NHS says eating too much saturated fat can raise "bad" LDL cholesterol, which can increase the risk of heart disease and stroke. Its limits are no more than 30g a day for men and 20g for women.

Foods high in saturated fat include fatty and processed meat (sausages, bacon, salami), butter, ghee and lard, cheese (especially hard cheese like cheddar), cream, cakes, biscuits and pastries, chocolate, and coconut and palm oil. The British Heart Foundation says the biggest sources in UK diets are red and processed meat (15%), cakes and biscuits (10%) and cheese (9%).

Eggs and shellfish are less of a worry than people think: the BHF says dietary cholesterol doesn't have a big impact on blood cholesterol for most people, though people with familial hypercholesterolaemia may need to limit it. How you cook eggs matters more.

On labels, the NHS says more than 5g of saturates per 100g is high and 1.5g or less is low. The NHS also says there's good evidence that replacing saturated fats with some unsaturated fats, such as olive or rapeseed oil, nuts and oily fish, can help lower cholesterol.
