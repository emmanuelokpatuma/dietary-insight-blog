Where: r/hypertension, or Quora: "Is there a lot of salt in bread and breakfast cereal?"

Yes, more than most people expect. Diabetes UK lists bread and breakfast cereals among the main sources of salt in our diets, and the recommended maximum for adults is 6g a day (about one teaspoon).

The British Heart Foundation says one slice of thick, wholemeal bread can contain as much as 2g of salt, a third of the daily limit. It also says some cereals, even those making health claims, contain a surprising amount, often added for flavour and shelf life. In its cereal ranking, a 30g serving of cornflakes has 0.34g of salt and bran flakes 0.2g, while plain porridge oats and no-added-salt muesli have almost none.

On labels, the BHF says more than 1.5g of salt per 100g is high and 0.3g or less is low. Diabetes UK suggests choosing foods that are green or amber for salt rather than red, shopping around because salt varies between products, and looking for reduced-salt versions.

If you have diabetes, Diabetes UK notes salt doesn't affect blood glucose, but too much can raise blood pressure, so it's still worth limiting.
