## community_answer
Where: r/diabetes_t2, or Quora: "What are some good low GI foods for diabetics?"

GI (glycaemic index) ranks carb foods from 0 to 100 by how fast they raise blood sugar. Diabetes UK counts 55 or below as low, 56 to 69 as medium and 70 or more as high.

Low GI examples from Diabetes UK's list: apples, pears, oranges, berries, carrots, broccoli, spinach, beans, peas, lentils, steel-cut oats, rye bread, yam, plantain and unsweetened milk and yoghurt. High GI includes white bread, potatoes, bagels, sweets and sugary drinks.

A few things worth knowing:

- Low GI doesn't mean healthy. Chocolate is low GI mainly because of its fat.
- Portion size often matters more. The BDA says it usually has the biggest effect on blood glucose, so a big plate of low GI pasta can still raise your sugar a lot.
- Preparation changes GI. Pasta cooked al dente is lower than softer pasta, and the BDA says cooling starchy foods or freezing or toasting bread lowers GI.
- Eating carbs with protein, fat, fibre and veg slows the rise.

If you take diabetes medication, check with your GP, diabetes nurse or dietitian before making big changes to your diet.
