**Eating out with a food allergy in the UK** starts with one rule: restaurants, cafés and takeaways must tell you if any of the 14 major allergens are in their food. The rest is up to you and the staff, so plan ahead, ask clear questions every time you order and carry your emergency medicine if you've been prescribed it.

## What do UK restaurants have to tell you about allergens?

The Food Standards Agency (FSA) says food businesses must tell you if they use any of the 14 regulated allergens as ingredients. Our guide to the [14 major food allergens](/14-major-food-allergens-uk/) explains what they are and where they hide.

The information can be on the menu, or the menu can tell you how to get it, for example by asking staff. For loose food, such as from a bakery or deli counter, the FSA says the business can tell you verbally, but it must signpost where the information can be found.

Anaphylaxis UK adds that businesses don't have to publish a full ingredients list. They do need a system that gives accurate information for every item containing the 14 allergens.

## What should you do before you book?

A little planning makes the meal itself much calmer. Anaphylaxis UK and Allergy UK both suggest you:

1. **Look at the menu online** and pick one or two dishes that might suit you.
2. **Phone ahead**, ideally speaking to a manager or head chef. Ask how the food is prepared and how they control cross-contamination.
3. **Ask whether the person you spoke to will be there** when you eat.
4. **Choose a quieter time**, such as early evening or a weekday. Allergy UK says chefs are more likely to make a mistake when the restaurant is busy.
5. **Think about the type of food.** Both charities say seafood restaurants carry a higher risk for fish or shellfish allergy, and some Asian cuisines carry a higher risk for nut allergy.
6. **Avoid buffets and salad bars**, where utensils are shared, unless you're served first.

If the risks feel high on a particular occasion, Anaphylaxis UK says it may be best to bring your own food or eat somewhere else.

## What should you ask staff when you order?

The NHS advises telling staff at restaurants and cafés about your allergy. Be clear and give examples of the foods that cause a reaction, the FSA suggests. Useful questions include:

| Ask about | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Ingredients in the dish | Allergens can be hidden in complicated recipes, the FSA says |
| Sauces, glazes, garnishes and cooking oil | These can add allergens you wouldn't expect |
| Recipe changes or substitutions | Allergy UK says recipes can change without warning |
| Shared pans, griddles and fryers | Anaphylaxis UK says woks and frying pans may not be cleaned thoroughly |
| Bread, rolls and desserts | Rolls can contain nuts or seeds |

Anaphylaxis UK gives some examples of hidden allergens: sauces may contain wheat flour, and some curries may be thickened with peanut flour or ground almonds. Allergy UK suggests asking the chef to use clean pans, clean utensils and fresh oil.

Allergy UK lists simple dishes as lower-risk choices, such as plain grilled or roast meat without marinades or sauces, steamed vegetables and potatoes, and fresh fruit for dessert. Even then, ask about every part of the plate.

## What if you're not confident in the answers?

If staff don't seem to understand your needs, the FSA says ask for the manager. If you still have doubts, the FSA's advice is simple: do not eat there.

When your food arrives, check it again before eating. Allergy UK says to send it back if you see or smell an allergen, and to ask for a freshly made meal. Removing the ingredient from the same plate isn't enough.

It's also worth remembering that a dish you've eaten before isn't automatically fine next time. Anaphylaxis UK points out that recipes vary between restaurants and even within the same one.

## How do allergen rules work for takeaways and delivery?

The FSA says that when you order food at a distance, such as by phone or online, allergen information must be available to you at two stages: when you order, and when the food is delivered. If you're ordering for several people, ask the business to label each meal and container.

Anaphylaxis UK recommends phoning the restaurant directly rather than relying on notes or messages passed on through apps. Our guide to [healthy takeaway options](/healthy-takeaway-options-diabetes/) has more tips on choosing what to order.

## What should you take with you?

The NHS says to carry 2 adrenaline auto-injectors or nasal sprays with you at all times, if they've been prescribed for you. Anaphylaxis UK also suggests having an up-to-date allergy action plan. Both charities say at least one person you're eating with should know about your allergy, and Allergy UK adds they should know how to use your device.

Anaphylaxis UK says alcohol, tiredness, infections and poorly treated asthma can raise the risk of a serious reaction, so factor these in when you plan a night out.

## What should you do if you have a reaction?

**Call 999 straight away** if lips, mouth, throat or tongue suddenly swell, breathing becomes fast or difficult, the throat feels tight or it's hard to swallow, skin, tongue or lips turn blue, grey or pale, or someone becomes suddenly confused, drowsy or dizzy, or faints. These can be signs of anaphylaxis.

If you have an adrenaline auto-injector or nasal spray, the NHS says to use it immediately, then call 999 for an ambulance, even if you or the person you're with seems to feel better. Do not drive yourself to A&E. Anaphylaxis UK adds: tell someone straight away, stay where you are and follow your emergency plan.

If you think you may have a food allergy that hasn't been diagnosed, the NHS says to see a GP. If a business didn't meet the allergen rules, the FSA says you can report it to the local authority.

## Checking a dish before you ask

Before you order, the Dietary Insight app can flag ingredients in a photo of a dish that may contain allergens on your profile, which can give you a starting point for your questions. It can't see hidden ingredients or cross-contamination, so always ask staff and check the full label.
