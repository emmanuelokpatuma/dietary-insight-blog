A good breakfast for type 2 diabetes combines **slow-release carbs, fibre and some protein**. That mix tends to raise blood sugar more gently and keeps you full until lunch. There's no special "diabetes diet", so these are ordinary foods you can buy in any UK supermarket.

## What makes a breakfast diabetes-friendly

- **Low-GI starchy foods:** oats, wholegrain or multigrain bread, and wholegrain cereal instead of white bread or sugary cereal.
- **Fibre:** adults should aim for 30g a day, and breakfast is an easy place to start.
- **Protein:** eggs, plain yoghurt, milk, nuts or seeds help you stay full.
- **Little or no free sugar:** watch the honey, syrup, jam, juice and flavoured yoghurts.

## 12 breakfast ideas

### Quick (under 5 minutes)

1. **Porridge with berries.** Make it with milk or water and add a handful of berries and a pinch of cinnamon instead of sugar.
2. **Plain Greek yoghurt with fruit and seeds.** Choose unsweetened yoghurt and add berries, sliced apple or a spoon of seeds.
3. **Wholegrain toast with peanut butter.** Use a thin spread of a no-added-sugar peanut butter.
4. **Wholegrain cereal with milk.** Pick a cereal with green or amber for sugar, and check the per 100g figure.
5. **Melon or grapefruit with unsweetened yoghurt.** A light option Diabetes UK suggests.
6. **Overnight oats.** Oats soaked in milk overnight, topped with berries in the morning.

### When you have more time

7. **Scrambled or poached eggs on wholegrain toast.**
8. **Vegetable omelette.** Peppers, spinach, mushrooms and onions.
9. **Beans on wholegrain toast.** Choose reduced-sugar and reduced-salt baked beans.
10. **Smoked salmon with egg.** Keep it as a treat, because smoked fish is salty.
11. **Shakshuka.** Eggs baked in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce.
12. **Wholegrain pancakes.** Top with berries and yoghurt instead of syrup.

## Breakfast traps to watch

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Sugary or chocolate cereal | Porridge or a plain wholegrain cereal |
| Fruit juice or a smoothie | A piece of whole fruit and a glass of water |
| White toast with jam | Wholegrain toast with eggs or peanut butter |
| Flavoured fruit yoghurt | Plain yoghurt with fresh berries |
| Pastries and croissants | Overnight oats or a vegetable omelette |

Juice and smoothies count as **free sugars**, and they have less fibre than whole fruit. A glass of juice can raise blood sugar quickly, even with "no added sugar" on the label.

## Portions still matter

A big bowl of a healthy cereal is still a lot of carbohydrate. Use a measured portion for a week to learn what one looks like. If you use insulin or medicines that can cause low blood sugar, talk to your diabetes team before changing your breakfast routine.
