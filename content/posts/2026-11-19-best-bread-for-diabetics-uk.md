**What is the best bread for diabetics in the UK?** Diabetes UK suggests choosing granary, pumpernickel or rye bread instead of white and wholemeal bread, because they tend to raise blood sugar more slowly. Wholegrain, seeded and multi-grain breads also add fibre. Whatever bread you choose, the amount you eat matters most.

## Can you eat bread if you have diabetes?

Yes. Diabetes UK lists bread alongside rice, pasta, potatoes, yams, plantain, breakfast cereals and couscous as starchy foods. These contain carbohydrate, which affects your blood sugar, but they're also part of a balanced diet.

Diabetes UK says the total amount of carbohydrate you eat will have the biggest effect on your glucose levels after eating. So it's less about cutting bread out and more about choosing a better type and keeping an eye on portions.

## Which breads have a lower GI?

The glycaemic index (GI) is a ranking from 0 to 100 that shows how quickly a carbohydrate food raises your blood sugar. Diabetes UK groups foods like this:

| GI score | What it means |
|---|---|
| 55 or below | Low: raises blood sugar slowly |
| 56 to 69 | Medium: raises blood sugar at a medium rate |
| 70 or more | High: raises blood sugar quickly |

Diabetes UK puts breads into these groups:

| Bread | Diabetes UK GI group |
|---|---|
| Rye bread | Low |
| Chapatti | Medium |
| White bread, bagels and some types of wholemeal bread | High |

Its advice is: "Instead of white and wholemeal bread, choose granary, pumpernickel or rye bread." Diabetes UK also says some mixed grain breads that include wholegrains have a lower GI than wholemeal or white bread.

The whole grains in these breads act as a physical barrier that slows down how quickly carbohydrate is absorbed. Wholemeal flour has been ground, so it doesn't have quite the same effect. For a wider list, see our [low GI foods list](/low-gi-foods-list-uk/).

## Is wholemeal bread better than white?

For fibre and heart health, yes. Diabetes UK says choosing wholegrains instead of refined carbs, such as white bread, is better for heart health. It suggests swapping white bread for wholemeal and choosing wholemeal, seeded or multi-grain bread because these are higher in fibre than white varieties.

But wholemeal isn't automatically low GI. Diabetes UK lists some types of wholemeal bread as high GI, which is why it points to granary, pumpernickel, rye and some mixed grain breads as lower-GI choices.

Diabetes UK says adults should aim for 30g of fibre a day, but the average adult in the UK has only around 19g. Fibre-rich bread is one easy way to close that gap. Diabetes UK recommends increasing fibre gradually and speaking to your healthcare professional before changing your diet.

## How do I read a bread label?

Bread names can be confusing. Here's what Diabetes UK says the common terms mean:

1. **Wholemeal, wholewheat and wholegrain:** basically different terms for the same thing, and all are wholegrain.
2. **Brown:** not as high in fibre as wholemeal.
3. **Granary:** contains malted wheat flakes, and may or may not be made from wholemeal flour, so check the label.
4. **Wheat-germ:** made from white flour with some wheat germ added back, so it is not wholegrain.

Diabetes UK suggests looking for the words "wholemeal", "wholewheat" and "wholegrain" on the label.

### Check the salt too

If you also have high blood pressure, salt matters. Blood Pressure UK says most of the salt we eat is hidden in foods we buy ready-made, like bread and breakfast cereals. It gives the example of two similar-looking loaves where one contains 1g of salt per slice and the other contains half that.

Blood Pressure UK says adults should eat less than 6g of salt a day. Use these per 100g guides on the label to compare loaves:

| Salt per 100g | Blood Pressure UK rating |
|---|---|
| 0.3g or less | Low |
| 0.3g to 1.5g | Medium |
| 1.5g or more | High |

Our guide on [how much salt a day](/how-much-salt-a-day-high-blood-pressure/) has more tips.

## How much bread should I eat?

There isn't one right amount for everyone. Diabetes UK says wholegrains are still carbohydrate, so be mindful of your portion sizes. On the GI page, it also advises spreading carbohydrate evenly across the day rather than eating large portions in one go.

For a sense of a wholegrain serving, Diabetes UK gives examples including:

1. 1 large slice (40g) of multi-grain bread
2. 1 slice of rye bread
3. 1 wholemeal pitta bread

If you take insulin or medicines that can cause hypos, talk to your GP, diabetes nurse or dietitian before changing how much carbohydrate you eat.

## What can I put with bread to help?

What you eat alongside bread matters too. Diabetes UK says protein and fat both lower the GI of a food, and protein, fat and fibre slow down the digestion and absorption of carbohydrate.

A few practical ideas:

1. Pair bread with some protein, such as eggs or beans.
2. Add salad or vegetables to a sandwich for extra fibre.
3. For chapattis, Diabetes UK suggests eating wholemeal chapatti and including dhal in your meals.
4. Try freezing your loaf. Diabetes UK lists frozen and defrosted bread as a swap for fresh bread, because cooling starchy foods creates more resistant starch.

If you'd like to understand GI and portions in more detail, Diabetes UK suggests speaking to your GP about a referral to a registered dietitian.

## Checking meals when there's no label

A sandwich from a café or a homemade flatbread doesn't come with a nutrition label. The Dietary Insight app can estimate the sugar, salt and fat in a meal from a photo and suggest a healthier swap. It's an estimate, so use it alongside food labels and your healthcare team's advice.
